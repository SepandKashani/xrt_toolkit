API Reference¶

The XTK API operates on Dr.Jit arrays, on CPU (drjit.llvm) or GPU (drjit.cuda). Volumes are flattened C-ordered coefficient arrays on a UniformSpec lattice. Rays are (anchor, direction) array pairs. asarray() converts NumPy, CuPy, PyTorch and JAX arrays.

X-Ray Transforms¶

xrt_toolkit.xrt_apply(ray_spec, knot_spec, order, data, buffer=None, weight=None)[source]¶

Compute 2D/3D projections.

Let \(f: \bR^{D} \to \bR\) such that

\[f(\bbx) = \sum_{\bbq \in \discreteRange{\bbZero, \bbQ-1}} f_{\bbq} \psi(\bbx - \bbx_{\bbq}),\]

with

\[f_{\bbq} \in \bR, \qquad \bbx_{\bbq} = \bbx_{0} + \bbq \odot \bbDelta, \qquad \bbx_{0} \in \bR^{D}, \quad \bbDelta \in \bR_{+}^{D},\]

and \(\psi\) a box spline with direction vectors \(\bbE = [\bbe_{1}, \ldots, \bbe_{N}] \in \bR^{D \times N}\), of unit integral.

Then xrt_apply() computes samples of

\[\xrt[f](\bbn, \bbt) = \int_{\bR} f(\bbt + \alpha \bbn) d\alpha\]
Parameters:

  • ray_spec (tuple[ArrayNfT, ArrayNfT]) – (L,) ray anchors \(\bbt \in \bR^{D}\) and directions \(\bbn \in \bR^{D}\).

  • knot_spec (UniformSpec) – Volume properties \((\bbx_{0}, \bbDelta, \bbQ)\).

  • order (0 | 1 | 2) –

    Data interpolation order.

    This parameter sets which \(\psi\) is used to interpolate data values:

    • order = 0 (2D, 3D):

      \[\bbE = \diag(\bbDelta)\]

    • order = 1 (2D):

      \[\begin{split}\bbE = \begin{bmatrix} \Delta_{1} & 0 & \Delta_{1} \\ 0 & \Delta_{2} & \Delta_{2} \end{bmatrix}\end{split}\]

    • order = 2 (2D):

      \[\begin{split}\bbE = \begin{bmatrix} \Delta_{1} & 0 & \Delta_{1} & \Delta_{1} \\ 0 & \Delta_{2} & \Delta_{2} & -\Delta_{2} \end{bmatrix}\end{split}\]

    • order = 1, 2 (3D): tensor-product B-splines (trilinear, triquadratic)

      \[\psi(\bbx) = \frac{1}{\Delta_{1} \Delta_{2} \Delta_{3}} \beta^{k}(x_{1} / \Delta_{1}) \beta^{k}(x_{2} / \Delta_{2}) \beta^{k}(x_{3} / \Delta_{3}), \quad \beta^{k} = \mathbb{1}_{[-1/2, 1/2]}^{\ast (k+1)}, \quad k = \text{order}\]

    The support of \(\psi\) and its projections can be viewed using plot_2d_basis().

  • data (FloatT) – (Q1,…,QD) flattened C-ordered volume weights \(f_{\bbq} \in \bR\).

  • buffer (FloatT) – (L,) buffer in which to accumulate projections.

  • weight (TOFSpec | PolynomialWeightSpec | TabulatedWeightSpec) – Weight along each ray: the line integral becomes \(\int_{\bR} f(\bbt + \alpha \hat{\bbn}) w(\alpha) d\alpha\), with \(\alpha\) the arc length from the anchor. (See TOFSpec, PolynomialWeightSpec, TabulatedWeightSpec.)

Returns:

proj – (L,) projections \(\xrt[f] \in \bR\).

Return type:

FloatT

xrt_toolkit.xrt_adjoint(ray_spec, knot_spec, order, data, buffer=None, weight=None)[source]¶

Compute 2D/3D back-projections.

Adjoint of xrt_apply(): maps projection weights to volume expansion coefficients. It is the exact transpose, \(\langle A f, y \rangle = \langle f, A^{T} y \rangle\) up to rounding: the same rays meet the same basis functions with the same weights.

Parameters:

  • ray_spec (tuple[ArrayNfT, ArrayNfT]) – (L,) ray anchors \(\bbt \in \bR^{D}\) and directions \(\bbn \in \bR^{D}\).

  • knot_spec (UniformSpec) – Volume properties \((\bbx_{0}, \bbDelta, \bbQ)\).

  • order (0 | 1 | 2) –

    Data interpolation order.

    This parameter sets which \(\psi\) is used to interpolate data values. The support of \(\psi\) and its projections can be viewed using plot_2d_basis().

  • data (FloatT) – (L,) projections \(g_{l} \in \bR\).

  • buffer (FloatT) – (Q1,…,QD) flattened buffer in which to accumulate back-projected weights \(f_{\bbq} \in \bR\).

  • weight (TOFSpec | PolynomialWeightSpec | TabulatedWeightSpec) – Weight along each ray: the line integral becomes \(\int_{\bR} f(\bbt + \alpha \hat{\bbn}) w(\alpha) d\alpha\), with \(\alpha\) the arc length from the anchor. (See TOFSpec, PolynomialWeightSpec, TabulatedWeightSpec.)

Returns:

xrt_toolkit.xrt_grad(ray_spec, knot_spec, order, data)[source]¶

Gradients of 2D/3D projections with respect to the ray geometry.

For each ray \(l\), the gradients of its projection \(P_{l}\) (see xrt_apply()) with respect to its anchor \(\bbt_{l}\) and its direction \(\bbn_{l}\):

\[\nabla_{\bbt} P_{l} = \int_{\bR} \nabla f(\bbt_{l} + \alpha \hat{\bbn}_{l}) \, d\alpha, \qquad \nabla_{\bbn} P_{l} = \frac{\bbI - \hat{\bbn}_{l} \hat{\bbn}_{l}^{T}}{\|\bbn_{l}\|} \int_{\bR} \alpha \nabla f(\bbt_{l} + \alpha \hat{\bbn}_{l}) \, d\alpha,\]

with \(\hat{\bbn}_{l} = \bbn_{l} / \|\bbn_{l}\|\). All D components come from one walk along the ray.

Parameters:

  • ray_spec (tuple[ArrayNfT, ArrayNfT]) – (L,) ray anchors \(\bbt\) and directions \(\bbn\). (See xrt_apply().)

  • knot_spec (UniformSpec) – Volume properties \((\bbx_{0}, \bbDelta, \bbQ)\).

  • order (0 | 1 | 2) – Interpolation order.

  • data (FloatT) – (Q1,…,QD) flattened C-ordered volume weights \(f_{\bbq} \in \bR\).

Returns:

grad_t, grad_n – (L,) gradients \(\nabla_{\bbt} P_{l}\), \(\nabla_{\bbn} P_{l}\), in the units of knot_spec.

Return type:

ArrayNfT

Notes

  • \(P_{l}\) depends neither on the anchor’s position along the line nor on \(\|\bbn_{l}\|\): \(\nabla_{\bbt} P_{l}\) and \(\nabla_{\bbn} P_{l}\) are orthogonal to \(\bbn_{l}\). Rotations pivot at the anchor.

  • Order 0: \(\nabla f\) lives on the cell faces. Each face the ray crosses contributes the jump of \(f\) across it, divided by \(\hat{n}_{k}\) (face normal to axis \(k\)), times \(\alpha\) for \(\nabla_{\bbn}\).

  • A ray through a lattice edge or corner (several faces crossed at once) is a kink of \(P_{l}\). There, the jump across face \(k\) is averaged over face \(k\) crossed first and crossed last: the gradient is what central finite differences converge to.

  • Orders 1, 2: in 3D, \(\nabla f\) is integrated over each polynomial piece (exact). In 2D, the closed-form projection of each basis function is differentiated: the anchor moves its distance to the line; the direction moves that distance (about the anchor) and the closed form itself.

  • 2D, order 1, rays along a lattice axis through basis centers: kinks of \(P_{l}\), where the gradient is again the average of both sides (along a diagonal, rounding makes it one-sided).

  • 2D, orders 1, 2, float32: within about 0.01 rad of a lattice axis or diagonal, the closed form cancels and \(\nabla_{\bbn} P_{l}\) loses accuracy (relative error 1e-4 at 0.01 rad, 3e-3 at 0.002 rad); float64 is exact.

Structured Rays¶

xrt_toolkit.xrt_struct_apply(ray_spec, knot_spec, order, data, buffer=None, weight=None)[source]¶

Compute 2D/3D structured projections.

Let \(f: \bR^{D} \to \bR\) such that

\[f(\bbx) = \sum_{\bbq \in \discreteRange{\bbZero, \bbQ-1}} f_{\bbq} \psi(\bbx - \bbx_{\bbq}),\]

with

\[f_{\bbq} \in \bR, \qquad \bbx_{\bbq} = \bbx_{0} + \bbq \odot \bbDelta, \qquad \bbx_{0} \in \bR^{D}, \quad \bbDelta \in \bR_{+}^{D},\]

and \(\psi\) a box spline with direction vectors \(\bbE = [\bbe_{1}, \ldots, \bbe_{N}] \in \bR^{D \times N}\), of unit integral.

Then xrt_struct_apply() computes samples of

\[\xrt[f](\bbn, \bbt) = \int_{\bR} f(\bbt + \alpha \bbn) d\alpha\]
Parameters:

  • ray_spec (tuple[ArrayNNfT, ArrayNNfT, UniformSpec]) –

    (L,) implicit ray descriptors \(\bbt \in \bR^{D}\) and directions \(\bbn \in \bR^{D}\).

    Expected arguments are:

    • ray_t_spec: ArrayNNfT (N_proj, D, D) homogeneous transforms \(\bbH_{t} = [\bbA_{t} \in \bR^{D \times (D-1)}, \bbb_{t} \in \bR^{D}]\).

    • ray_n_spec: ArrayNNfT (N_proj, D, D) homogeneous transforms \(\bbH_{n} = [\bbA_{n} \in \bR^{D \times (D-1)}, \bbb_{n} \in \bR^{D}]\).

    • ray_u_spec: UniformSpec D-dim uniform mesh.

    Let \(\bbu \in \bR^{D}\) be a point on the mesh specified by ray_u_spec. Then ray_spec encodes rays of the form

    \[\bbt(\bbu) = \bbH_{t} \bbu, \bbn(\bbu) = \bbH_{n} \bbu.\]

    ray_spec encodes L = N_proj * prod(ray_u_spec.num) projections.

  • knot_spec (UniformSpec) – Volume properties \((\bbx_{0}, \bbDelta, \bbQ)\).

  • order (0 | 1 | 2) –

    Data interpolation order.

    This parameter sets which \(\psi\) is used to interpolate data values:

    • order = 0 (2D, 3D):

      \[\bbE = \diag(\bbDelta)\]

    • order = 1 (2D):

      \[\begin{split}\bbE = \begin{bmatrix} \Delta_{1} & 0 & \Delta_{1} \\ 0 & \Delta_{2} & \Delta_{2} \end{bmatrix}\end{split}\]

    • order = 2 (2D):

      \[\begin{split}\bbE = \begin{bmatrix} \Delta_{1} & 0 & \Delta_{1} & \Delta_{1} \\ 0 & \Delta_{2} & \Delta_{2} & -\Delta_{2} \end{bmatrix}\end{split}\]

    • order = 1, 2 (3D): tensor-product B-splines (trilinear, triquadratic)

      \[\psi(\bbx) = \frac{1}{\Delta_{1} \Delta_{2} \Delta_{3}} \beta^{k}(x_{1} / \Delta_{1}) \beta^{k}(x_{2} / \Delta_{2}) \beta^{k}(x_{3} / \Delta_{3}), \quad \beta^{k} = \mathbb{1}_{[-1/2, 1/2]}^{\ast (k+1)}, \quad k = \text{order}\]

    The support of \(\psi\) and its projections can be viewed using plot_2d_basis().

  • data (FloatT) – (Q1,…,QD) flattened C-ordered volume weights \(f_{\bbq} \in \bR\).

  • buffer (FloatT) – (L,) buffer in which to accumulate projections. The (N_proj, *ray_u_spec.num) mesh-points are serialized in C-order.

  • weight (TOFSpec | PolynomialWeightSpec | TabulatedWeightSpec) – Weight along each ray (see xrt_apply()), per ray in the order of the projections. Arc lengths are measured from the anchors of struct_rays().

Returns:

proj – (L,) projections \(\xrt[f] \in \bR\).

Return type:

FloatT

xrt_toolkit.struct_rays(ray_spec)[source]¶

Individual rays of a structured geometry.

Returns one ray (anchor \(\bbt\), direction \(\bbn\)) per (projection, detector cell) of a geometry from parallel_beam() or cone_beam(), in the order of xrt_struct_apply()’s output. With these rays, xrt_apply() / xrt_adjoint() give the same results as xrt_struct_apply() / xrt_struct_adjoint().

Parameters:

Return type:

Notes

The rays are computed lazily: used directly in a projector, they are evaluated inside its kernel and never stored.

Weights Along Rays¶

class xrt_toolkit.TOFSpec(center, sigma)[source]¶

Time-of-flight (TOF) weight along each ray, a Gaussian.

As weight of xrt_apply(), it gives

\[\int_{\bR} f(\bbt + \alpha \hat{\bbn}) \, g_{\sigma}(\alpha - \mu) \, d\alpha, \qquad g_{\sigma}(\alpha) = \frac{1}{\sigma \sqrt{2\pi}} \exp\left(-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right),\]

where \(\alpha\) is the signed arc length from the ray anchor \(\bbt\) along the unit direction \(\hat{\bbn}\). In positron emission tomography, \(\mu\) is the most likely annihilation position along the line of response and \(\sigma\) the timing resolution of the scanner, both in the length unit of knot_spec.

Parameters:

  • center (FloatT | float) – (L,) TOF centers \(\mu\), one per ray, or a scalar shared by all rays.

  • sigma (FloatT | float) – (L,) TOF standard deviations \(\sigma > 0\), one per ray, or a scalar shared by all rays.

Notes

  • Order 0 is exact: the window is integrated over each voxel chord.

  • 3D orders 1, 2 integrate the window with the basis functions by Gauss quadrature: float32 accurate for \(\sigma\) of one voxel or more.

  • 2D orders 1, 2 weight each basis function by the window at its center. This is an approximation, with relative error decreasing like 1/\(\sigma\) (order 1: 1.4% at \(\sigma\) = 3 pixels, 0.13% at 30).

  • Binned TOF data is modelled by repeating each ray once per TOF bin, with that bin’s center.

class xrt_toolkit.PolynomialWeightSpec(coeff, origin=0.0)[source]¶

Polynomial weight along each ray,

\[w(\alpha) = \sum_{k=0}^{K-1} c_{k} (\alpha - \alpha_{0})^{k},\]

where \(\alpha\) is the signed arc length from the ray anchor along the unit direction. Pass it as weight of xrt_apply() and xrt_adjoint().

Parameters:

  • coeff (list[FloatT | float]) – Coefficients \(c_{0}, \ldots, c_{K-1}\), in increasing degree. Each is a scalar shared by all rays, or (L,) one per ray.

  • origin (FloatT | float) – Expansion point \(\alpha_{0}\), scalar or (L,).

Notes

  • Order 0 is exact.

  • 3D orders 1, 2 are exact for degrees up to 8 (order 1) and 5 (order 2).

  • 2D orders 1, 2 weight each basis function by \(w\) at its center (an approximation).

Examples

Multiple-scattering moments of a ray of length \(H\), anchored at its entry point (muon scattering tomography):

>>> m0 = PolynomialWeightSpec([1.0])  # int f
>>> m1 = PolynomialWeightSpec([H, -1.0])  # int f (H - alpha)
>>> m2 = PolynomialWeightSpec([H * H, -2.0 * H, 1.0])  # int f (H - alpha)^2
class xrt_toolkit.TabulatedWeightSpec(values, num, start=0.0, step=1.0)[source]¶

Weight along each ray, tabulated on a uniform grid of arc lengths and interpolated linearly.

Sample \(j\) of ray \(l\) sits at arc length \(\text{start}_{l} + j \, \text{step}_{l}\) from the ray anchor. Beyond the ends of the table, the weight is constant. Pass it as weight of xrt_apply() and xrt_adjoint().

Use it when the weight has no closed form, e.g. the attenuation factor \(\exp(-\int_{\alpha}^{\infty} \mu)\) of an attenuated transform with diverging rays.

Parameters:

  • values (FloatT) – (L * num,) weights, ray-major: ray \(l\) holds values[l * num : (l + 1) * num].

  • num (int) – Samples per ray, at least 2.

  • start (FloatT | float) – Arc length of sample 0, scalar or (L,).

  • step (FloatT | float) – Sample spacing > 0, scalar or (L,).

Notes

  • Order 0 is exact: the interpolant is integrated over each voxel chord.

  • 3D orders 1, 2 integrate it with the basis functions by Gauss quadrature, which does not see the kinks of the interpolant: accurate when the table is smooth over a knot step (relative error 2e-4 for random values every 4 knot steps, 2e-2 every quarter step).

  • 2D orders 1, 2 weight each basis function by the interpolant at its center (an approximation).

xrt_toolkit.fbp(ray_spec, knot_spec, y, window='hann')[source]¶

Filtered backprojection of a parallel-beam scan.

Parameters:

  • ray_spec (RaySpecT) – Scan from parallel_beam(), with views covering \([0, \pi)\) uniformly. 2D, or 3D (each detector row reconstructs one slice).

  • knot_spec (UniformSpec) – Reconstruction lattice, centered on the rotation axis.

  • y (FloatT) – (L,) projections, in the order of xrt_struct_apply().

  • window ("hann" | "hamming" | "cosine" | "shepp-logan" | "ramp") – Apodization of the ramp filter (“ramp”: none).

Returns:

f – (Q1,…,QD) flattened C-ordered volume coefficients, as the data of xrt_apply(); 0 outside the field of view (voxels that some view does not see).

Return type:

FloatT

Notes

With a detector finer than the lattice, the filter stops at the lattice resolution, as if the detector cells were binned to the voxel size: a finer detector never makes the reconstruction noisier, but it does not make it sharper either.

xrt_toolkit.from_astra(proj_geom, vol_geom, Array=None)[source]¶

Convert an ASTRA geometry pair into XTK rays and volume lattice.

The returned (ray_spec, knot_spec) can be passed directly to xrt_apply() and xrt_adjoint(). The rays are ordered as the flattened ASTRA sinogram: (angles, det) in 2D, (det_v, angles, det_u) in 3D. Convert volume arrays with vol_astra_to_xtk() and vol_xtk_to_astra().

Supported geometries: parallel, fanflat, parallel3d, cone and their *_vec forms. Only the non-*_vec forms need astra-toolbox to be installed.

Parameters:

  • proj_geom (dict) – ASTRA projection geometry, e.g. from astra.create_proj_geom().

  • vol_geom (dict) – ASTRA volume geometry, e.g. from astra.create_vol_geom(). Voxels must be isotropic.

  • Array (ArrayNfT type) – Ray coordinate type. (Default: drjit.cuda.Array2f or drjit.cuda.Array3f.)

Returns:

  • ray_spec (tuple[ArrayNfT, ArrayNfT]) – (L,) ray anchors \(\bbt\) and directions \(\bbn\).

  • knot_spec (UniformSpec) – Volume lattice.

Notes

Lengths are in units of the voxel size \(s\): XTK’s projections are ASTRA’s divided by \(s\).

xrt_toolkit.interop.vol_astra_to_xtk(vol)[source]¶

Reorder an ASTRA volume array into XTK’s flattened C-ordered layout.

ASTRA stores 2D volumes as (rows, cols) = (-y, x), the row index increasing towards -y, and 3D volumes as (slices, rows, cols) = (z, y, x). XTK stores a flat C-ordered array indexed by (x, y[, z]), all axes increasing.

Parameters:

vol (np.ndarray) – (ny, nx) or (nz, ny, nx) ASTRA volume.

Returns:

flat – (nx * ny [* nz],) XTK coefficients.

Return type:

np.ndarray
